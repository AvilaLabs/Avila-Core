# Counter-positioning and the underdog advantage

Counter-positioning is not “having features an incumbent lacks.” It exists only
when Avila adopts a model that a capable incumbent would resist because copying
it damages the incumbent’s existing economics, control, or channel.

The positions below are hypotheses. They become real only through public
commitments and product behavior.

## The proposed position

| Core commitment | Customer value | Why an incumbent may resist |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Customer-owned, portable evidence | No dependence on an Avila subscription to inspect prior work | Weakens proprietary data and workflow lock-in |
| Provider- and solver-neutral routing | Best admissible capability can win each contract | Reduces preference for the vendor’s own solver portfolio |
| Contract pricing independent of verdict | Trust that `FAIL` and `INCONCLUSIVE` are economically honest | Conflicts with vendors selling success services or favorable throughput narratives |
| Deliberate compute minimization | Lower cost and faster selective reruns | Conflicts with revenue tied to core-hours, cloud consumption, or job volume |
| Independent local verifier | Evidence consumers do not have to trust Avila’s screen | Relinquishes control over interpretation and ongoing access |
| Free local execution boundary | Bottom-up use and air-gapped compatibility | Cannibalizes seat, platform, or hosted-access models |
| External professionals own and monetize methods | Broader specialist supply; authority remains visible | Shares economics and control with small providers |
| Public qualification scope and limitations | Buyers can compare admissibility, not brand alone | Exposes method boundaries vendors may prefer to package opaquely |

Any commitment Avila routinely waives for a large customer is not a strategic
position.

## The sacrifices that make it credible

Avila must be willing to:

- let a third-party capability win over an Avila capability;
- let customers retain and verify evidence after ending a commercial relationship;
- support local execution where Avila earns no compute markup;
- charge the same completion fee for `PASS`, `FAIL`, and `INCONCLUSIVE`;
- publish limitations that may reduce short-term sales;
- minimize runs even when more jobs would create more revenue elsewhere;
- make the viewer/verifier independently implementable;
- give capability authors meaningful economics; and
- refuse unsupported work instead of disguising custom consulting as platform
  coverage.

These choices narrow easy revenue paths. That is why they could create trust and
make replication costly.

## Turning present disadvantages into design advantages

### Avila does not own a major solver

Therefore it can be a neutral referee. The architecture should make it technically
obvious when an Avila implementation is selected and allow a policy to forbid
self-preference.

### Avila does not own a cloud

Therefore it can route to the customer’s existing environment and optimize away
compute. The evidence contract—not infrastructure consumption—becomes the billable
unit.

### Avila is not expert in every supported field

Therefore Core must encode professional ownership rather than founder authority.
Capability manifests should name method owners, reviewers, scope, evidence, and
limitations. External experts become the supply side of the product, not advisory
decoration.

### Avila has no large installed base

Therefore it can choose open boundaries and new economics without migrating
thousands of legacy customers. It can also abandon a wrong schema before it
becomes institutional infrastructure.

### Avila has limited resources

Therefore it must solve one full decision loop, not build shallow support for
many disciplines. Existing tools are wrapped rather than rewritten. Public
benchmarks, transparent failures, and design-partner work substitute for a large
brand and sales force.

### Avila has no recognized assurance brand

Therefore it cannot ask users to “trust Avila.” It must make evidence portable,
signed, inspectable, reproducible, and countersignable by people the customer
already trusts.

## What would destroy the position

- making the best experience require Avila-hosted execution;
- hiding evidence behind a paid viewer;
- creating a proprietary evidence format with no independent verifier;
- ranking providers by commercial payments instead of declared policy;
- allowing an interface status to outrun its evidence;
- making professional services the permanent path for every contract;
- expanding to many fields before one repeatable contract works; or
- branding Core primarily around fashionable implementation technology rather
  than the durable trust problem.

## Test for every major feature

Ask:

1. Does this make a contract faster or cheaper to resolve without weakening its
   evidence policy?
2. Does it strengthen portability, provider neutrality, or independent review?
3. Does it compound across future contracts?
4. Would an incumbent rationally hesitate to copy the associated economic
   commitment?

If the answer to all four is no, the feature is unlikely to build the intended
advantage.
